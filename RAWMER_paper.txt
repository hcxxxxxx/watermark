==================================================================
RAWMER: Reference-Assisted Speech Watermarking via
Relative Mel-Energy Relations
==================================================================
(Draft for ICASSP 2027 -- 4 pages text + 1 page references)


------------------------------------------------------------------
ABSTRACT
------------------------------------------------------------------
Neural text-to-speech and voice cloning create a pressing need to verify
whether a suspect utterance originates from a given generation service. We
target a reference-assisted setting in which the provider retains a compact
reference of the clean mel spectrogram at generation time and later checks
whether a suspect clip matches a claimed payload, key, and record. Within this
setting, prior mel-domain watermarking encodes the payload as an absolute
perturbation pattern whose residual is fragile under strong additive noise. We
propose RAWMER, which instead encodes each payload bit as a relative energy
relation between two reliable groups of mel bins within short time blocks, so
that detection relies on a differential score that cancels common-mode
distortion. A reliability-aware selection picks boundary-safe, energy-stable,
and balanced bin pairs from the clean reference, and the payload is embedded
redundantly across blocks for robust voting. On LJSpeech with HiFi-GAN and
DiffWave, RAWMER attains 0.9999/0.9998 bit accuracy on clean watermarked speech
(PESQ 3.51/3.54) and, at quality matched to a re-implemented MelShield baseline,
improves 5 dB-SNR accuracy from 0.6328 to 0.8587 under HiFi-GAN and from
0.7173 to 0.8308 under DiffWave. Negative
controls yield <= 0.4% false verification for wrong key/payload/reference,
references compress to about 22 KB per utterance, and 25% clips remain
verifiable under blind crop search. The main limitation is strong
time/frequency desynchronization such as large speed or pitch changes.


------------------------------------------------------------------
I. INTRODUCTION
------------------------------------------------------------------
Modern text-to-speech (TTS) and voice cloning systems synthesize speech that is
difficult to distinguish from genuine recordings, raising the risk of fraud,
impersonation, and misinformation and motivating mechanisms that trace audio
back to its source [REF: synthetic-speech-risk]. Audio watermarking, which
embeds an imperceptible payload that survives later processing, has become a
central tool for such provenance attribution [REF: audioseal, wavmark].

Most TTS systems map text to an intermediate mel spectrogram and then to a
waveform through a vocoder such as HiFi-GAN [REF: hifigan] or DiffWave
[REF: diffwave]. Because the mel spectrogram is a shared, model-agnostic
interface across these pipelines, recent work has moved keyed, in-generation
watermarking from the waveform into the mel domain: MelShield [REF: melshield]
treats the intermediate mel as the host signal and embeds a keyed payload via
low-energy spread-spectrum perturbations before vocoding, so that a provider who
retains the clean reference mel can later verify ownership without retraining the
vocoder. This mel-domain, reference-assisted design reports strong robustness
under common signal-processing distortions -- near-perfect decoding under MP3 and
AAC compression and over 95% accuracy at 20 dB additive noise -- while preserving
perceptual quality. We adopt the same setting: at generation time the provider
stores a compact clean-mel reference, and given a suspect clip it checks whether
the clip matches a claimed payload, key, and record.

Our point of departure is the encoding mechanism. MelShield, like prior
mel-domain schemes, represents each bit as an absolute perturbation pattern and
detects it by correlating the reference residual with the keyed pattern. Such
absolute residuals are sensitive to overall gain, frequency shaping, and vocoder
reconstruction error, and their separability degrades markedly under strong
distortion: at lower signal-to-noise ratios the embedded pattern is buried and
bit accuracy falls toward chance. This fragility under strong additive noise,
rather than under common compression or filtering, is the problem we target.

Our central observation is that, within a short time block, the relative energy
relation between two groups of mel bins is more stable than the absolute residual
of any single bin: gain changes, mild filtering, and local reconstruction bias
act in common on a local region and largely cancel in a differential measurement.
We propose RAWMER, which encodes each payload bit as the sign of an energy
difference between a positive and a negative group of mel bins, repeated across
time blocks, and a reliability-aware selection that uses the clean reference to
choose boundary-safe, energy-stable, and balanced bin pairs.

We make three contributions:

(1) Relative mel-energy watermarking. We formulate the payload as block-wise
relative energy relations between mel-bin groups rather than absolute
perturbation patterns, yielding a differential detection score robust to
common-mode distortion.

(2) Reliability-aware pair selection. We use the clean reference to pick
boundary-safe, energy-stable, and balanced positive/negative pairs from multiple
candidates, which we show is the main source of robustness under strong additive
noise.

(3) A systematic study of the setting. Across HiFi-GAN and DiffWave, RAWMER
improves additive-noise robustness over a quality-matched MelShield
re-implementation, while negative controls, references compressed to about 22 KB
per utterance, and blind partial-clip verification show the setting is sound and
practical. We also report a candid comparison with waveform-domain baselines and
disclose the main limitation, strong time/frequency desynchronization.


------------------------------------------------------------------
II. PROPOSED METHOD
------------------------------------------------------------------

A. Problem Setting: Reference-Assisted Verification
----------------------------------------------------
At generation time, the provider holds a clean mel spectrogram
M in R^{F x T}, min-max normalized to [0,1], where F is the number of mel bins
and T the number of frames.
For each utterance it draws a payload b in {0,1}^L of L bits, derived
deterministically from an utterance identifier and a secret key. The secret key
is kept in protected verifier state, while the retained reference record stores
the utterance identifier, a payload commitment or recomputable payload metadata,
and a (possibly compressed) copy of the clean mel M. RAWMER embeds b into M to
produce a watermarked mel M_hat, which a vocoder G renders to the released
waveform x_hat = G(M_hat).

Verification is payload-specific rather than mere presence detection. Given a
suspect clip y, the verifier recovers the expected payload b from the
identifier and key, re-analyzes y to a mel M_y, and forms the residual against
the retained reference,

        R = M_y - M,                                                   (1)

from which it decodes b_hat. Writing the bit accuracy as
acc = (1/L) sum_i 1[b_hat_i = b_i], the clip is verified if acc >= tau for a
threshold tau. Attribution is decided by this payload match, not by signal
strength alone, so a correct decision requires the correct combination of key,
payload, and reference. The threshold is set below 1 (we use tau = 0.75) to
tolerate the few bit errors expected under strong distortion, while the negative
controls of Section III show that wrong key/payload/reference still fail.

B. Relative Mel-Energy Encoding
-------------------------------
Rather than writing a bit as the presence of an expected absolute perturbation,
RAWMER writes it as the sign of an energy difference between two groups of mel
bins. The time axis is partitioned into blocks of W frames with stride W
(non-overlapping; W = 8 frames in our configuration), and embedding is confined
to a watermark band of mel bins [f_lo, f_hi) (we use bins 20-60 of 80). Each
block carries K payload bits (K = 6), so a single block contributes a small,
redundant slice of the full payload.

For a given bit assigned to a block, RAWMER selects two disjoint sets of mel
bins within the band, a positive group P and a negative group N, each of size p
(p = 6). The bit is embedded by pushing the two groups in opposite directions
inside the block region B (the W frames by the bins of P and N):

  - to embed 1: raise the energy of P and lower the energy of N;
  - to embed 0: lower the energy of P and raise the energy of N.

Perturbations are scaled by a strength alpha and constrained by a spectral mask
(floor mask_floor) and a boundary margin so that the modified mel stays within
its admissible range and audible damage is limited.

At detection, RAWMER does not inspect any single bin in M_y; it reads the
residual R of (1), removes its global mean (R <- R - mean(R)) to discard any
uniform offset between the suspect and the reference, and compares the two
groups,

        s \propto mean_{(f,t) in P x B} R(f,t)
              - mean_{(f,t) in N x B} R(f,t).                          (2)

In the implementation, this comparison is computed as the mean inner product
between the block residual and a signed, unit-normalized pair vector, whose
positive entries correspond to P and negative entries correspond to N.

A positive score s favors bit 1, a negative score favors bit 0. Because s is a
difference of two co-located group means computed on a DC-removed residual, any
distortion that acts in common on the local block region -- a gain change, mild
filtering, or a slowly varying vocoder reconstruction bias -- shifts both terms
together and largely cancels, leaving the embedded directional offset. This
differential measurement is the source of RAWMER's robustness relative to
absolute-pattern detection.

C. Reliability-Aware Pair Selection
-----------------------------------
Not every pair of bin groups is equally trustworthy. Bins with very low energy
are easily ignored by the vocoder or buried by noise; bins already close to the
admissible boundary cannot be pushed further without clipping or mask
truncation; and if P and N differ greatly in reliability, the score s becomes
biased. RAWMER therefore does not commit to a single random pair. For each
bit/block it generates C candidate pairs (C = 16) and scores them against the
clean reference using two notions of reliability:

  - Boundary reliability: bins farther from the admissible upper/lower bound can
    absorb the intended perturbation with less clipping and less audible damage.
  - Energy reliability: bins carrying nontrivial speech energy propagate the
    perturbation through the vocoder more stably than near-silent bins.

The goal is not to pick the loudest bins but to pick a pair in which both groups
are reliable and the two are balanced against each other. With rel(P) and rel(N)
the mean reliabilities of the two groups, a candidate is scored as

  score = min(rel(P), rel(N)) - 0.25 * | rel(P) - rel(N) |,           (3)

so that a pair reliable on both sides is preferred over one that is strong on
one side and weak on the other, and the most reliable candidate is selected for
embedding. Here reliability combines a boundary term, proportional to the
headroom min(M, 1-M) of a bin toward its admissible bounds, and an energy term
that grows with the bin's speech energy. As shown in Section III, increasing C
from 1 to 16 is the single largest contributor to additive-noise robustness, at
negligible cost in clean speech quality.

D. Block-Wise Embedding and Voting
----------------------------------
The payload is not written once but distributed redundantly across many time
blocks, and the per-block evidence is aggregated at detection. This block-wise
design has several consequences that matter for forensic use. It resists local
corruption, since blocks spared by noise, echo, or cropping still carry the
payload; it supports partial-clip verification, since any fragment retaining
enough blocks can be decoded; and it stabilizes the decision through voting,
since averaging many noisy per-block scores yields a more reliable bit estimate
than any single block. For each payload bit, the signed scores of (2) from all
blocks that encode it are accumulated into a vote, and the sign of the
accumulated vote gives the decoded bit b_hat_i. When only a fragment is
available and its offset in the reference is unknown, the verifier slides the
fragment over the reference (search step of 4 frames) and keeps the alignment
that maximizes agreement, which Section III shows localizes the crop to within
about one mel frame.


------------------------------------------------------------------
III. EXPERIMENTS
------------------------------------------------------------------

A. Experimental Setup
---------------------
Dataset and vocoders. We evaluate on LJSpeech, embedding watermarks in the mel
domain and rendering waveforms with two vocoders, HiFi-GAN [REF: hifigan] and
DiffWave [REF: diffwave], so that robustness is measured across distinct
synthesis mechanisms. The main RAWMER/MelShield comparison uses 2000 random
utterances with seed 2026; waveform-domain baselines and ablations use 500
utterances unless stated otherwise. Mel spectrograms use F = 80 bins, and the
watermark band is bins 20-60.

Configuration. The payload is L = 32 bits and the verification threshold is
tau = 0.75. RAWMER uses blocks of W = 8 frames with stride 8, K = 6 bits per
block, group size p = 6, and C = 16 reliability-ranked candidates per bit.
The embedding strength is alpha = 0.435 for HiFi-GAN and alpha = 0.35 for
DiffWave, with mask floor 0.20 and boundary margin 0.01; the DiffWave sampler
uses a fixed seed.

Metrics. We report bit accuracy (ACC), the per-bit match between the decoded and
expected payload, and verification rate (VR), the fraction of clips with
ACC >= tau. Watermark imperceptibility is measured by PESQ and STOI of the
watermarked (or attacked) audio against an unwatermarked benchmark; we control
imperceptibility through the no-attack PESQ. Note that PESQ under strong
additive noise is low by construction, as the attack itself heavily degrades the
signal.

Baselines. MelShield [REF: melshield] is the closest baseline: mel-domain and
reference-assisted, but built on absolute perturbation patterns. We compare
against it in two ways. We cite the values reported in the MelShield paper as an
external reference, and we additionally re-implement and tune it to match 
RAWMER's clean PESQ, so that the two methods are compared
under one code base, vocoder, attack set, and metric. The reported and
re-implemented numbers are kept separate and never merged into a single
"fair" protocol. AudioSeal [REF: audioseal] and WavMark [REF: wavmark] are strong
waveform-domain baselines of a different paradigm; we report them separately
(Section III-C) and do not treat them as same-protocol comparisons, since they
embed 16-bit payloads on the waveform whereas the mel-domain methods embed
32 bits.

B. Robustness vs. MelShield
---------------------------
Table 1 reports the main comparison against MelShield across both vocoders. On
clean watermarked speech, RAWMER reaches ACC 0.9999 (HiFi-GAN, PESQ 3.51) and
0.9998 (DiffWave, PESQ 3.54) with VR 1.000, on par with MelShield's clean
accuracy. The difference appears under strong additive noise. For DiffWave, at
clean PESQ matched almost exactly (RAWMER 3.5419 vs. MelShield 3.5475), RAWMER
improves accuracy from 0.9661 to 0.9898 at 20 dB SNR, from 0.8180 to 0.9194 at
10 dB, and from 0.7173 to 0.8308 at 5 dB -- gains of 2.4, 10.1, and 11.3
percentage points. The HiFi-GAN comparison shows the same trend: at PESQ 3.51,
RAWMER attains 0.9942/0.9408/0.8587 at 20/10/5 dB, against a quality-matched
re-implementation at 0.9028/0.7129/0.6328 (PESQ 3.51). The MelShield paper's
reported figures, included in Table 1 as an external reference, follow a similar
pattern of degradation at 10 and 5 dB. Under non-noise attacks (MP3, AAC,
scaling, resampling, band-/low-pass, echo), both methods remain at or near VR
1.000, so the relative-energy formulation does not trade away common-attack
robustness to gain additive-noise robustness.

  Vocoder   Method                 Clean PESQ   none    noise20  noise10  noise5

  HiFi-GAN  MelShield (re-impl.)      3.5100    0.9993  0.9028   0.7129   0.6328

  DiffWave  MelShield (re-impl.)      3.5475    0.9998  0.9661   0.8180   0.7173
  DiffWave  RAWMER (ours)             3.5419    0.9998  0.9898   0.9194   0.8308

C. Comparison with Waveform-Domain Baselines
--------------------------------------------
AudioSeal and WavMark belong to a different paradigm -- post-hoc waveform
watermarking with detector- or decoder-based recovery of a 16-bit payload -- so
Table 2 is a cross-paradigm reference rather than a same-protocol comparison.
Both baselines are very strong on non-noise attacks and introduce little clean
quality loss (clean PESQ above 4.0). Under strong additive noise, however, their
verification collapses. AudioSeal keeps high message bit accuracy at 20 dB
(0.9736 on HiFi-GAN) but its detector rejects most clips, giving VR 0.304
(HiFi-GAN) and 0.138 (DiffWave), and VR 0.000 at 10 and 5 dB. WavMark, reported
with forced decoding, falls to near-chance accuracy at 10/5 dB (0.5114/0.4926 on
HiFi-GAN), and its high-level decode rate is essentially zero under these noise
levels. Thus, within our reference-assisted setting and under strong additive
noise, RAWMER verifies far more reliably than these waveform baselines, while we
do not claim superiority on the non-noise attacks or clean quality where they
remain strong.

  bit accuracy / verification rate. Cross-paradigm reference; 500 utterances.

  HiFi-GAN  AudioSeal         4.4574       0.9736/0.304   0.7900/0.000   0.6465/0.000
  HiFi-GAN  WavMark (forced)  4.2975       0.7523/0.630   0.5114/0.046   0.4926/0.034

D. Validating the Reference-Assisted Setting
--------------------------------------------
A reference-assisted method must show that it does not pass for an arbitrary
reference, and that retaining references is practical. Table 3 summarizes three
studies on HiFi-GAN.

False positives. We run negative controls on 2000 utterances with no attack.
The correct combination of reference, key, and payload verifies at VR 1.000
(ACC 0.9999), whereas unmarked audio, a wrong key, a wrong payload, and a wrong
reference all drop to chance accuracy (ACC ~ 0.50) with VR of 0.40%, 0.15%,
0.40%, and 0.35%, respectively. Notably, the wrong-payload control retains a
confidence value equal to the positive case yet fails verification, confirming
that attribution must rest on payload match rather than signal strength alone.

Reference compression. Storing the full float32 mel costs 174.96 KB per
utterance. Quantizing only the watermark band (bins 20-60) to 8 bits reduces
this to 21.87 KB -- one eighth of the cost -- while preserving robustness:
ACC/VR of 0.9939/1.000, 0.9394/0.993, and 0.8584/0.925 at 20/10/5 dB,
essentially matching the float32 reference. A same-size full-mel 4-bit reference
drops more strongly, showing that preserving precision in the watermark band is
preferable to uniformly quantizing all mel bins. This makes per-utterance
reference storage realistic for a service provider.

Aggressive reference compression. We further compare RAWMER and MelShield under
more aggressive quantization on 500 utterances. RAWMER remains robust with only
the watermark band stored at 4 bits (11.05 KB): ACC/VR is 0.9826/1.000,
0.9128/0.984, and 0.8245/0.856 at 20/10/5 dB. At a comparable 11.05 KB,
MelShield's full-mel 2-bit reference gives 0.7859/0.982, 0.6554/0.710, and
0.6007/0.442, while MelShield band-only references degrade even more. This
suggests that, in our re-implementation, MelShield depends on a more complete
reference, whereas RAWMER's relation-based signal can be localized to the
watermark band and quantized more aggressively.

Blind partial-clip verification. To avoid assuming a known crop position, the
verifier slides each fragment over the reference (search step 4 frames) and
keeps the best-scoring alignment. A 25% fragment still verifies at ACC
0.985-0.991 with VR 0.998-1.000, and a 50% fragment at ACC >= 0.997 with VR
1.000; the recovered crop offset has a median error of about one mel frame.
Block-wise redundancy thus distributes watermark evidence evenly enough to
support short, unaligned clips.

E. Ablation and Limitations
---------------------------
Pair selection. Table 4 isolates the effect of reliability-aware selection on
HiFi-GAN. Increasing the candidate count C from 1 (a single random pair) to 16
raises noise robustness sharply -- by 4.9, 17.7, and 19.2 points at 20/10/5 dB
-- while clean PESQ drops only from 3.5485 to 3.5102. Reliability-aware pair
selection is therefore the principal source of RAWMER's additive-noise
robustness, at negligible perceptual cost. Across seeds 2026-2028 the main
configuration is stable (noise5 ACC 0.8607/0.8588/0.8574).

  TABLE 4. Pair-candidate ablation on HiFi-GAN, 500 utterances.

  C    Clean PESQ   none    noise20  noise10  noise5
  ---  ----------   ------  -------  -------  ------
  1    3.5485       0.9999  0.9459   0.7674   0.6684
  4    3.5299       0.9999  0.9873   0.8894   0.7845
  8    3.5184       0.9999  0.9918   0.9232   0.8315
  16   3.5102       0.9999  0.9952   0.9439   0.8607

Robustness scope and limitations. Beyond the common attacks of Table 1, RAWMER
stays robust under modern operations: low-bitrate MP3/AAC, 2 kHz low-pass, wide
band-pass, amplitude clipping, 8-bit quantization, and strong reverberation all
keep VR at 1.000 on both vocoders. Two boundaries remain. Extreme 0 dB additive
noise lowers VR to 0.486-0.598. More importantly, RAWMER is sensitive to
time/frequency desynchronization: detection relies on frame-level alignment
between the suspect and reference mels, so large speed changes and pitch shifts
misalign the blocks and shift the chosen bin relations. Mild distortions are
tolerated -- pitch shifts within +/-50 cents keep ACC above 0.995 and VR 1.000,
and +/-3% speed keeps VR above 0.99 -- but a one-semitone (+/-100 cents) shift
drops ACC to about 0.78, and +/-10% speed drops VR below 0.33. The dominant
failure mode is not loss of watermark energy but broken synchronization, which
points to multi-scale alignment, dynamic time warping, or pitch-aware matching
as future work.


------------------------------------------------------------------
IV. CONCLUSION
------------------------------------------------------------------
We presented RAWMER, a reference-assisted mel-domain watermarking method for
provenance verification of neural speech. Rather than encoding payload bits as
absolute perturbation patterns, RAWMER writes them as relative energy relations
between reliability-selected groups of mel bins across repeated time blocks, and
detects them with a differential, DC-removed score that cancels common-mode
distortion. On LJSpeech with HiFi-GAN and DiffWave, RAWMER substantially
improves additive-noise robustness over a quality-matched MelShield
re-implementation while remaining strong under common signal-processing attacks.
A systematic study of the setting -- low false verification under
wrong-key/payload/reference controls, compact ~22 KB references, and blind
partial-clip verification -- indicates that reference-assisted verification is
both sound and practical for provider-side attribution. The main limitation is
strong time/frequency desynchronization, such as large speed or pitch changes;
addressing it through multi-scale alignment, dynamic time warping, or
pitch-aware matching is our primary direction for future work.
